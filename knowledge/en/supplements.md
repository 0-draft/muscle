## Bottom line

- Take **3–5 g/day of creatine monohydrate** every day. Timing doesn't matter
- Take **210–420mg** of caffeine (3–6 mg/kg) 60 minutes before training. For evening sessions, use a dose that doesn't cut into your sleep
- If your protein intake is adequate, you don't need BCAAs or EAAs

## Creatine

> [!A] Adding creatine to resistance training adds **about 1.1–1.5kg** of lean mass. It works as well in trained lifters as in beginners.

- In a meta-analysis of 61 trials, lean mass increased by +1.39kg (95% confidence interval 1.07–1.70), with no difference between trained lifters and beginners (Ashtary-Larky 2025)
- Other meta-analyses agree: +1.14kg (Desai 2024) and +1.46kg in men (Delpino 2022)
- A 2026 meta-analysis of 63 studies also found a positive effect on lean mass (Wang 2026)

> [!B] When muscle thickness is measured directly, though, the effect is **small**. Part of the lean mass gain is water inside the muscle.

A meta-analysis of regional hypertrophy found a small effect size of 0.11 (95% credible interval −0.02 to 0.25) (Burke 2023). It is still cheap and has extensive safety data, so it is well worth taking.

> [!A] Long-term intake of 3–5g a day is considered safe.

The ISSN position stand considers long-term intake safe for healthy people (Kreider 2017). Loading (about 20g/day for the first 5–7 days) only makes the effect show up sooner. You can skip it.

## Caffeine

> [!A] **3–6 mg/kg** of caffeine gives a small but reliable boost to strength and power.

- In a meta-analysis, the effect size was 0.20 for strength and 0.17 for power (Grgic 2018)
- An umbrella review of 21 meta-analyses also found a consistent effect (Grgic 2020)
- The ISSN recommends 3–6 mg/kg about 60 minutes before training. The minimum effective dose is about 2 mg/kg (Guest 2021)

> [!D] There is no evidence that it directly increases hypertrophy. At most, it may help indirectly by improving session quality.

## Weak evidence or unnecessary

> [!D] If protein intake is adequate, adding BCAAs or EAAs does nothing. HMB has mixed results in trained lifters. Herbal testosterone boosters have no good evidence.
