## Bottom line

- Sleep **7–9 hours every night**, on a consistent schedule
- Avoid heavy drinking after training. The effect of occasional moderate drinking has not been studied
- Aim for 3–5 g/kg of carbs (210–350g). Don't let fat drop below 0.5 g/kg (35g)

## Sleep

> [!B] Adults need **at least 7 hours** of sleep.

Consensus of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the Sleep Research Society (Watson 2015).

> [!C] Sleep loss visibly lowers muscle protein synthesis in the short term.

- One night of total sleep deprivation: muscle protein synthesis −18%, cortisol +21%, testosterone −24% (Lamon 2021)
- Five nights of 4-hour sleep lowered myofibrillar protein synthesis from 1.53 to 1.24 %/day. High-intensity interval exercise brought it back (Saner 2020)
- One short night barely affects strength, but several in a row reduce output on multi-joint lifts (Knowles 2018)

No RCT has restricted sleep for months and measured hypertrophy directly. The evidence so far is mechanistic.

## Alcohol

> [!C] Drinking **heavily** after training lowers muscle protein synthesis by about 24%, even with protein.

1.5g of alcohol per kg of body weight (about 105g at 70kg, roughly 10 drinks) caused −24%. With carbs instead of protein, it was −37% (Parr 2014). The study had 8 subjects and a single session. There is no data on regular moderate drinking.

## Carbs and fat

> [!C] The minimum carb intake for hypertrophy is unknown. Extra carbs improved lifting performance mainly when training fasted or in long sessions of more than 10 sets per muscle.

Systematic review of 49 studies (Henselmans 2022). 3–5 g/kg is given as a guideline (Iraki 2019).

> [!B] Cutting fat drastically lowers testosterone.

In a meta-analysis, low-fat diets lowered total testosterone (effect size −0.38, Whittaker 2021). Whether a small change within the normal range affects hypertrophy is unknown, but 0.5 g/kg is a safe floor.
